閱讀測驗

📄 基隆高中 · 第二次期中考 · L4-閱讀-stress
A recent study published in PLOS ONE has provided scientific evidence that dogs can distinguish between the odors of humans who are stressed and those who are calm. Earlier research indicated that dogs might also detect emotions like happiness and fear, but this study focused on minimizing competing scents and accurately measuring human stress levels. Researchers collected breath and sweat samples from participants as a baseline, then had them perform a mentally taxing task—counting backward from 9,000 by 17s—while monitoring their stress responses. Following this task, additional breath and sweat samples were collected, along with heart rate and blood pressure data. Thirty-six participants who reported feeling stressed had their samples presented to dogs. In the experiment, 20 dogs were tasked with identifying the correct post-task samples, and they needed to select the right one at least seven out of ten times to progress. In the final phase, the four dogs that succeeded in the first phase were again tested and needed to identify the stress scent at least 80% of the time for conclusive results. The dogs correctly identified the stress odor in 93.8% of trials, highlighting their remarkable ability to differentiate between these scents linked to psychological stress responses.
